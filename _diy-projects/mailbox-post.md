The mailbox post is the first thing people see when they come to your home.

Most people never think about it. It is just a post in the ground with a box on top. I had one like that. It was plain. It looked like nobody cared about it. I decided to change that.

I built a custom timber post from scratch. The address numbers are carved directly into the wood with a router. They are not painted on a plate. They are not plastic letters screwed on. They are cut into the wood and filled white so they are easy to read from the street. The post has a cross-arm for the address sign on one side. The mailbox sits on top. The base sits in white river rock with black stone accents.

People slow down when they drive past. Neighbors have knocked on the door to ask about it. That is how you know you got it right.

## Step-by-Step

<div style="margin: 2rem 0;">
  <h3>1. The finished post</h3>
  <img src="/assets/images/diy/mailbox/after-overview.jpg" alt="The completed custom timber mailbox post installed at the front of the house" style="width:100%; border-radius:10px; margin:0.75rem 0 0.5rem 0;">
  <p>A custom timber post with the address carved into the wood. A cross-arm sign that reads 4913. A white mailbox on top. White river rock and black stone at the base. Here is how it came together.</p>
</div>

---

<div style="margin: 2rem 0;">
  <h3>2. Starting at the miter saw</h3>
  <img src="/assets/images/diy/mailbox/step-miter-saw.jpg" alt="Cutting the lumber to length on the miter saw" style="width:100%; border-radius:10px; margin:0.75rem 0 0.5rem 0;">
  <p>I cut the main post and arm pieces to rough length first. Getting the lengths right at the start is important. Once the post is in the ground, you cannot go back and trim it.</p>
</div>

---

<div style="margin: 2rem 0;">
  <h3>3. Ripping the post to width</h3>
  <img src="/assets/images/diy/mailbox/step-circular-saw.jpg" alt="Ripping the post to final width with a circular saw" style="width:100%; border-radius:10px; margin:0.75rem 0 0.5rem 0;">
  <p>I used the circular saw to cut the lumber down to the final width. Working on the floor with a long piece like this, you need a steady hand and a straight guide line.</p>
</div>

---

<div style="margin: 2rem 0;">
  <h3>4. Carving the numbers</h3>
  <img src="/assets/images/diy/mailbox/step-routing.jpg" alt="Routing the address numbers into the post with a Bosch router" style="width:100%; border-radius:10px; margin:0.75rem 0 0.5rem 0;">
  <p>I used the Bosch router to carve the address numbers — 4913 — directly into the face of the wood. Not a sticker, not a plate, cut right into the grain. There was no fixing a mistake once the bit touched the wood, so I was careful.</p>
</div>

---

<div style="margin: 2rem 0;">
  <h3>5. The numbers filled with epoxy</h3>
  <img src="/assets/images/diy/mailbox/step-numbers-closeup.jpg" alt="Close-up of the routed address numbers filled with white epoxy" style="width:100%; border-radius:10px; margin:0.75rem 0 0.5rem 0;">
  <p>After carving, I poured liquid white epoxy into the numbers and waited for it to harden. Epoxy fills the carved grooves cleanly and stays solid. Against the warm color of the wood the contrast is sharp. You can read them clearly from the street.</p>
</div>

---

<div style="margin: 2rem 0;">
  <h3>6. Finishing the surface</h3>
  <img src="/assets/images/diy/mailbox/step-finishing.jpg" alt="Wire brushing the post to open the grain before finishing" style="width:100%; border-radius:10px; margin:0.75rem 0 0.5rem 0;">
  <p>I wire brushed the whole post before any stain or sealer. It cleans up rough spots from the routing and gives the wood a nice texture. It is a slow step but it shows up in the final result.</p>
</div>

---

<div style="margin: 2rem 0;">
  <h3>7. The cross-arm cuts</h3>
  <img src="/assets/images/diy/mailbox/step-miter-saw-2.jpg" alt="Precision cuts on the miter saw for the cross-arm pieces" style="width:100%; border-radius:10px; margin:0.75rem 0 0.5rem 0;">
  <p>Back at the miter saw for the cross-arm pieces. The arm needs to sit level against the post. Even a small angle looks wrong when you see it outside every day, so I took my time.</p>
</div>

---

<div style="margin: 2rem 0;">
  <h3>8. Installed</h3>
  <img src="/assets/images/diy/mailbox/after-street.jpg" alt="The completed mailbox post from the street — side view" style="width:100%; border-radius:10px; margin:0.75rem 0 0.5rem 0;">
  <p>Seen from the street. The warm stain on the timber, the white mailbox on top, the sign arm reading 4913, and the gravel base with white and black stone. It looks like it belongs there.</p>
