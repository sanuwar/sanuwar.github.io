The mailbox post is the first thing people see when they come to your home.

Most people never think about it. It is just a post in the ground with a box on top. I had one like that. It was plain. It looked like nobody cared about it. I decided to change that.

I built a custom timber post from scratch. The address numbers are carved directly into the wood with a router. They are not painted on a plate. They are not plastic letters screwed on. They are cut into the wood and filled white so they are easy to read from the street. The post has a cross-arm for the address sign on one side. The mailbox sits on top. The base sits in white river rock with black stone accents.

People slow down when they drive past. Neighbors have knocked on the door to ask about it. That is how you know you got it right.

---

## Project Overview

**What it is:** A custom timber mailbox post with address numbers carved into the wood, a cross-arm sign, and a gravel base with decorative stone.

**Why it matters:** The mailbox post is the first thing anyone sees before they reach your front door. A plain post says you never thought about it. A built one says you did.

**What the build involved:** Cutting and milling the lumber, ripping the post to the right size with a circular saw, making precise cuts on the miter saw, routing the address numbers by hand, finishing the wood, and landscaping the base with decorative stone.

---

<div style="margin: 2rem 0;">
  <h3>2. Starting out — miter saw</h3>
  <img src="/assets/images/diy/mailbox/step-miter-saw.jpg" alt="Cutting the lumber to length on the miter saw" style="width:100%; border-radius:10px; margin:0.75rem 0 0.5rem 0;">
  <p>I started at the miter saw and cut the main post and arm pieces to rough length. Getting the lengths right at the start is important. Once the post is in the ground, you cannot go back and trim it.</p>
</div>

---

<div style="margin: 2rem 0;">
  <h3>3. Ripping the post to the right width</h3>
  <img src="/assets/images/diy/mailbox/step-circular-saw.jpg" alt="Ripping the post to final width with a circular saw" style="width:100%; border-radius:10px; margin:0.75rem 0 0.5rem 0;">
  <p>I used the circular saw to rip the lumber down to the final width I wanted for the post. Working on the floor with a long piece like this, you need a steady hand and a straight guide line. The Ryobi circular saw made clean cuts through it.</p>
</div>

---

<div style="margin: 2rem 0;">
  <h3>4. Router work — carving the numbers in</h3>
  <img src="/assets/images/diy/mailbox/step-routing.jpg" alt="Routing the address numbers into the post with a Bosch router" style="width:100%; border-radius:10px; margin:0.75rem 0 0.5rem 0;">
  <p>This is the step that makes the post special. I used the Bosch router to carve the address numbers — 4913 — directly into the face of the wood. Not a sticker. Not a plate. Cut right into the grain. The depth and spacing had to be right because there was no fixing a mistake once the bit touched the wood.</p>
</div>

---

<div style="margin: 2rem 0;">
  <h3>5. The numbers — carved and painted white</h3>
  <img src="/assets/images/diy/mailbox/step-numbers-closeup.jpg" alt="Close-up of the routed and white-painted address numbers" style="width:100%; border-radius:10px; margin:0.75rem 0 0.5rem 0;">
  <p>After carving, I painted the numbers white. Against the warm color of the wood, the contrast is sharp and clean. You can read them clearly from the street. No sign plate needed. No extra bracket. The wood itself carries the address.</p>
</div>

---

<div style="margin: 2rem 0;">
  <h3>6. Finishing the surface</h3>
  <img src="/assets/images/diy/mailbox/step-finishing.jpg" alt="Wire brushing the post to open the grain before finishing" style="width:100%; border-radius:10px; margin:0.75rem 0 0.5rem 0;">
  <p>Before adding any stain or sealer, I wire brushed the whole post to open the grain and clean up any rough spots from the routing. This step feels slow but it shows in the final result. It gives the wood a clean, textured look instead of a rough sanded surface.</p>
</div>

---

<div style="margin: 2rem 0;">
  <h3>7. Final cuts — the cross-arm pieces</h3>
  <img src="/assets/images/diy/mailbox/step-miter-saw-2.jpg" alt="Precision cuts on the miter saw for the cross-arm pieces" style="width:100%; border-radius:10px; margin:0.75rem 0 0.5rem 0;">
  <p>Back at the miter saw for the final cuts on the cross-arm. The arm needs to sit level and straight against the post. Even a small angle looks wrong once it is installed outside and you see it every day. I took my time on these cuts.</p>
</div>
