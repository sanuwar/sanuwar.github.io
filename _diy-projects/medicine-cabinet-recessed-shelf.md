The old medicine cabinet needed to go.

It had a mirrored door that squeaked every time you opened it. The shelves inside were narrow. You could barely stand a bottle on them. It worked, but it looked like something nobody had thought about in years. I finally decided to do something about it.

The hole in the wall was already there. I did not need to make it bigger or change anything structural. I just needed to use that space better. I built a tiled recessed shelf to go in its place. White subway tile on the back and sides. Two open shelves. A decorative hexagon tile border around the outside. No door. No mirror. Just a clean, open shelf built into the wall.

It took more steps than I expected. But the result was worth it.

---

## Project Overview

**What it is:** A tiled recessed shelf built into an existing medicine cabinet opening — white subway tile inside, two shelves, and a hexagon tile border around the frame.

**Why it matters:** The hole in the wall was the same. What I put there just needed more thought. A tiled shelf looks clean, lasts longer, and works better than a cheap cabinet with a squeaky door.

**What the build involved:** Removing the old cabinet, building a custom wood box to fit the opening, cutting and setting the subway tile, cutting the decorative hexagon border tile on a wet saw, grouting, and fitting the trim.

<div style="margin: 2rem 0;">
  <h3>2. Building the box — fitting it into the opening</h3>
  <img src="/assets/images/diy/medicine-cabinet/step-box-install.jpg" alt="Fitting the custom wood shelf box into the wall opening" style="width:100%; border-radius:10px; margin:0.75rem 0 0.5rem 0;">
  <p>Once the old cabinet was out, I built a wood box to fit the opening. It has four sides, a back panel, and two shelf supports. Getting the box to sit flat and straight in the opening was the first challenge. The old opening was not perfectly square, so I had to adjust and trim until it fit right.</p>
</div>

---

<div style="margin: 2rem 0;">
  <h3>3. Cutting tile — the wet saw in the garage</h3>
  <img src="/assets/images/diy/medicine-cabinet/step-tile-saw-1.jpg" alt="Cutting tiles on the RIDGID wet saw in the garage" style="width:100%; border-radius:10px; margin:0.75rem 0 0.5rem 0;">
  <p>With the box in place, I moved to the garage and set up the RIDGID wet saw. I needed to cut two types of tile — white subway tile for the inside of the shelf, and hexagon tile for the decorative border around the outside. Each cut has to be measured carefully. Tile does not forgive mistakes.</p>
</div>

---

<div style="margin: 2rem 0;">
  <h3>4. Getting every cut right</h3>
  <img src="/assets/images/diy/medicine-cabinet/step-tile-saw-2.jpg" alt="Close-up of tile being cut on the RIDGID wet saw" style="width:100%; border-radius:10px; margin:0.75rem 0 0.5rem 0;">
  <p>The border tile needed the most careful cuts. The hexagon pieces had to meet cleanly at each corner so the frame would look neat and finished. For cuts like these I measure twice, cut once, and then hold the piece up to check the fit before moving on.</p>
</div>

---

<div style="margin: 2rem 0;">
  <h3>5. Tile set inside — border tile next</h3>
  <img src="/assets/images/diy/medicine-cabinet/step-tiled-open.jpg" alt="The tiled interior with two shelves, raw wood frame still exposed around the opening" style="width:100%; border-radius:10px; margin:0.75rem 0 0.5rem 0;">
  <p>The white subway tile inside the box is done. The two shelves are in place. You can see how the space is divided and how much room each shelf gives. The raw wood frame around the outside is still showing here — the hexagon border tile goes on next.</p>
</div>

---

<div style="margin: 2rem 0;">
  <h3>6. Grouting the border tile</h3>
  <img src="/assets/images/diy/medicine-cabinet/step-grout.jpg" alt="Applying grout to the hexagon decorative tile border with blue gloves" style="width:100%; border-radius:10px; margin:0.75rem 0 0.5rem 0;">
  <p>Once the hexagon border tile dried, it was time to grout. You push the grout into the joints between tiles and then wipe off the extra. The white grout against the white hexagon tile gives a clean, simple look. It is not flashy — it just looks right.</p>
</div>

---

<div style="margin: 2rem 0;">
  <h3>7. Checking the trim fit</h3>
  <img src="/assets/images/diy/medicine-cabinet/step-trim-fit.jpg" alt="Checking and fitting the trim frame against the wall around the tiled shelf" style="width:100%; border-radius:10px; margin:0.75rem 0 0.5rem 0;">
  <p>Before finishing, I checked the trim all the way around the opening. Where the tile meets the wall, the line needs to look clean. A small gap in the wrong place makes the whole thing look unfinished. A little extra time here is always worth it.</p>
</div>

---

<div style="margin: 2rem 0;">
  <h3>8. Done — the finished shelf</h3>
  <img src="/assets/images/diy/medicine-cabinet/after.jpg" alt="The completed tiled recessed shelf with hexagon border tile and two shelves" style="width:100%; border-radius:10px; margin:0.75rem 0 0.5rem 0;">
  <p>Same hole in the wall. Completely different bathroom. The hexagon border frames the opening. The white tile inside is clean and bright. The two shelves hold exactly what needs to be there. No squeaky door. No mirror you never wanted. Just a shelf that looks like it was always supposed to be there.</p>
</div>
